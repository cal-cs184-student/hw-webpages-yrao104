Part 1: Ray Generation and Scene Intersection

Ray Generation + Primitive Intersection

To generate rays from the camera perspective, I first transformed the given image coordinates to the camera space using the formulas mapping the image coordinates to the sensor range. Specifically, I used the formulas for the bottom left corner and top right corner of the virtual camera sensor to help with this. Then, I transformed the ray to the world space by multiplying it by c2w, the camera-to-world rotation matrix. Lastly, I set the min_t and max_t values to nClip and fClip. Next, to generate pixel samples, I looped through ns_aa samples of camera rays and generated samples from the gridSampler. I then normalized the sample coordinates by the width and height of the image, estimated the global radiance, and finally averaged the cumulative value. Primitive intersection essentially samples the ray along the scene to find the closest intersection point from min_t to max_t.

Triangle Intersection Algorithm

I utilized the Moller Trumbore algorithm for triangle intersection. Using the formula, I calculated the scalar values for t, b_1, and b_2. To check whether the ray was in the triangle, I ensured that the denominator dot product between s_1 and e_1 was not equal to 0, b1 and b2 were >= 0, t was in the ray bounds, and b1 + b2 was <= 1. To calculate the surface normal of the intersection, I used the formula (1 - b_1 - b_2)*n_1 + b_1*n_2 + b_2*n_3.
